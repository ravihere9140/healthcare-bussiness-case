# CardioNova: Where Should the Sales Force Spend Its Calls?
**A pharma commercial analytics project (decision analytics case study)**

> Data is **synthetic** (generated by `01_generate_data.py`), modelled on a typical pharma HCP-targeting setup. Brand name, prices, call costs and the competitor launch are assumptions made for the exercise.

---

## 1. Business problem

A pharma client sells **CardioNova**, a branded hypertension drug. 2,500 physicians (HCPs) across 5 regions are visited by reps, emailed, sent samples and invited to events. Leadership asks:

1. How is the brand performing, and where is it losing ground?
2. Are calls going to the right doctors?
3. With the **same call budget**, what is the best allocation, and what is it worth?

## 3. Key findings

![trend](charts/fig1_trend.png)

**1. Revenue is flat while the market grows.** Last-12-month revenue was **$30.4M (+0.5% YoY)** while market Rx grew **+4.7%**. Brand share fell from **12.8% to 12.3%**.

![impact](charts/fig2_competitor_impact.png)

**2. The damage is concentrated in East and Central.** After the Dec-2025 competitor launch, share fell **-13% in East and -14% in Central** versus about -2% elsewhere. That is roughly **$1.6M of annualised revenue**, about 71% of it in those two regions.

![heatmap](charts/fig3_share_heatmap.png)

**3. Calls only partly follow potential.** Call intensity rises with doctor volume, but the highest-volume decile holds **25.6% of market potential and gets only 18.4% of calls**.

![misallocation](charts/fig4_misallocation.png)

![segments](charts/fig5_segments.png)

| Segment | HCPs | % of potential | % of calls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under-served (high potential, < 2.5 calls/month) | 360 | 18.3% | 11.3% |
| Over-served (low potential, >= 2.5 calls/month) | 220 | 6.5% | 13.3% |

**4. Returns to calls differ sharply by specialty, and they diminish.** Going from 1 to 4 calls a month lifts share by about **+32% for GPs** but only **+11% for cardiologists**. Cardiologists are among the most-called doctors yet the least responsive.

![response](charts/fig6_response_curves.png)

**5. About half of all calls do not pay for themselves.** The next call is worth roughly $66 of revenue for the median doctor versus an assumed $65 cost. **49% of doctors** sit below breakeven, and they receive **56% of all calls** (about 2,830 of 5,040 a month). Median marginal call value is $100 for GPs and $30 for cardiologists.

![marginal](charts/fig9_marginal_call_value.png)

**6. Channel economics.** Revenue returned per $1 spent: **email $4.3, call $0.7, samples $0.2, events $0.2**. Events are rare in the data, so that estimate is noisy.

![channels](charts/fig7_channels.png)

**7. A re-allocation with zero extra budget is worth real money.** The model keeps total calls fixed (about 5,040 a month), holds KOL call levels unchanged, and allows 1 to 6 calls per doctor.

| Scenario | Calls moved | Incremental Rx / year | Incremental revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full re-allocation | 1,385 / month (27%) | +9,454 (+3.5%) | **~$1.06M** |
| **Phased: 30% of the shift (recommended first step)** | 416 / month (8%) | +4,455 (+1.6%) | **~$0.50M** |

Most calls move from cardiologists and over-served doctors to under-served GPs and internal-medicine physicians.

![realloc](charts/fig8_reallocation.png)

**8. Early-warning watchlist.** 76 high-value doctors saw share fall more than 25% in the last 6 months, about 2,800 Rx a year at risk (~$0.3M). **51 of the 76 are in East and Central** and they already get more calls than average (2.4 vs 2.0), so more calls are unlikely to be the fix. See `outputs/watchlist_high_value_declining_hcps.csv`.

## 4. Recommendations

1. **Phase 1 (next quarter):** move ~8% of calls (about 415 a month) from over-served doctors and cardiologists to the 360 under-served GPs and IM physicians. Use `outputs/hcp_call_plan.csv`. Expected value is about $0.5M a year at no extra cost.
2. **Validate before scaling:** run it as a test-and-control pilot (some territories reallocated, matched territories unchanged) for 2 quarters before the full 27% shift.
3. **Stop low-yield calling:** for HCPs below breakeven, replace some in-person calls with email where the doctor has opted in.
4. **East and Central need a competitive response, not more calls:** counter-detailing messages, medical-science-liaison outreach and a retention plan for the 76 watchlist doctors.
5. **Review samples and events spend:** both show low measured return. Test before cutting, since events may carry brand effects not captured here.

## 5. Method and assumptions

- **Response curve:** per specialty, brand share vs ln(1 + calls) on the pre-launch period with region controls, giving a diminishing-returns curve. In-sample fit: modelled Rx 267k vs actual 271k.
- **Channel effectiveness:** HCP fixed-effects regression of share on calls, lagged calls, emails, samples, events and a post-launch dummy.
- **Optimiser:** equalises marginal Rx per call across HCPs (closed form with bisection on the budget constraint), with per-HCP bounds of 1 to 6 calls and KOLs fixed. Baseline share is shrunk 50% toward the specialty x region mean to avoid chasing noise.
- **Assumptions to confirm with the client:** $112 net revenue per Rx, call $65, email $2, sample $6, event $350, competitor launch date Dec-2025. Revenue is not margin.
- **Limitations:** response is estimated from cross-sectional differences in call intensity (not a randomised experiment), so some confounding is possible. Gains are modelled, not observed. Tier labels and potential are taken from the data as given. The call-per-doctor cap and relationship constraints are simplified.
